One study panel, three signals

The card shows its Dutch text with everything reachable at once: test your reading, hear the sentence spoken, look up the meaning, and give your own score. What you do before you score decides how much the app trusts that score.

The panel

This mock works. The four score buttons ask one thing: how easily did you recall this sentence? The panel never says when the card will come back or how a score will be counted. That happens out of sight.

Voorbeeldkaart

Today I am writing a Linux program.

Out of sight, never shown in the app: Weaker signal so far: a score with nothing to back it up. “Gaat goed” would bring this card back in 5 weken.

The example card has been practised three times and was last seen two weeks ago; the pauses come from the app's own scheduler. In the mock, “Test mijn uitspraak” skips the recording and shows an example result: grade 3, one word missed.

What each control does

Test mijn uitspraakSpatie Records you reading the card. Stops by itself when you finish, or on a second press. The app then shows a pronunciation grade from 1 to 4, the share it understood, and underlines the words it missed in the card text. The button becomes “Opnieuw” for another try; the latest try counts. “Mijn opname” beside the result plays your recording back (O). Makes the review count in full. A pronunciation grade below your own score replaces it.
Hoor de zinH Plays the sentence in the Dutch voice you chose in the settings. Available at any moment, as often as you like. L plays it slowly. None. Listening never changes how the review counts.
BetekenisV Shows the English, and the Dutch note if the card has one. Press again to hide. Looking once is remembered for this review even if you hide it again. Negative. The review counts as Moeilijk at best and the pause does not grow.
Score 1 to 41–4 Saves the review and shows the next card straight away. Available from the first moment, so you can skip speaking. Disabled only while a recording is running or being judged. Required. Alone, it counts at 70%.

The last column is for this plan only. The score buttons carry their name and key and nothing else: no pause, no “counts as” line. You mark how easily the sentence came back, and the app does the rest without comment. How scores are counted will be described in a user manual, written later and outside this plan.

How a review is counted

None of this appears on the panel. The scheduler (FSRS) takes a score from 1 to 4 and proposes a longer pause when the score is good. Two things are new: evidence can lower the score, and evidence decides how much of that longer pause you get.

  1. Start with your own score. Nothing is saved without it.
  2. Lower it where the evidence disagrees. If the app understood you less well than you scored yourself, the pronunciation grade is used (this is today's rule). If you opened the meaning, the score is Moeilijk at best.
  3. Grant a share of the longer pause. Spoke and scored: all of it. Scored only: 70% of the growth. Opened the meaning: none, the card returns after the same pause as last time.

“Weet ik niet” always counts in full, whatever else you did. Nobody claims a miss by accident, so it needs no evidence. A brand-new card has no pause to grow yet, so only rule 2 applies on its first review.

Build order

  1. The counting rules. One function in crates/core takes your score, the pronunciation grade if there is one, and whether the meaning was opened, and returns the score to use and the share to grant. It replaces combine in speech.rs. Tests cover every row of the tables above.
  2. The scheduler. schedule.rs applies the share to the growth FSRS proposes, when a review is saved. The four preview pauses are no longer worked out for the screen. store.rs saves the share with each review in a new column, added to existing deck files on open like the speech columns were.
  3. The icons. Three SVG files in crates/app/assets/icons, loaded ahead of the interface kit's built-in set in main.rs.
  4. The panel. Rebuild the card view in study.rs and fold the recording controls from speaking.rs into the test button: idle, recording with the level meter, judging, judged. Remove the step strip and the hear-after-scoring step. The voice clip is prepared when the card appears, so button 2 answers at once. Take the pause off the score buttons and drop the “Telt als” line.
  5. Check it for real. Run the app headless with a prepared recording through scripts/capture.sh and screenshot each state: fresh, recording, judged, meaning open, speech recognition not installed. Then update the README.

Steps 1 and 2 change scheduling without touching the screen, so they can be tested on their own before the panel is rebuilt.

Settled, and what I'll assume

The first point is decided. The other three go ahead as suggested unless you say otherwise.

  1. Settled: the voice. Button 2 plays the Dutch speaker chosen in the settings. Listening never affects the review, before or after you speak.
  2. Luisteren mode. It hides the text and plays the card, which doesn't fit this panel. My suggestion: leave it as it is, behind a two-way switch in the header.
  3. How hard the signals bite. My suggestion: 70% for score only, and for the meaning “Moeilijk at best, no longer pause”. Gentler would be dropping the no-growth part, so an opened meaning still grows the pause a little (2 weken becomes 3). Harsher would be treating it as “Weet ik niet”, which brings the card back in minutes.
  4. The Dutch labels. “Test mijn uitspraak”, “Hoor de zin”, “Betekenis”. My suggestion: these three, since the test judges how well you are understood. “Lees voor” is the alternative if you want it closer to “reading”.
