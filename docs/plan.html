Build plan · 19 November 2026

Dutch flashcards from your conversations

A small app on this laptop. You paste snippets from your Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini conversations, it drafts Dutch cards, you approve them, and it brings each card back at longer and longer gaps as you get it right. You read the card aloud, score yourself, then hear it.

What is settled

  • Dutch only. English is stored with each card but stays hidden until you ask for it.
  • This laptop only. It runs locally in its own desktop window.
  • You grade yourself with four buttons. No AI listens to or judges your speech.
  • Cards come from pasted snippets. Claude Code turns them into drafts, using your subscription and no API key.
  • Nothing enters the deck unreviewed. Every draft passes through an edit screen first.

How a card travels

  1. PlakkenYou paste a piece of conversation into the Add screen.
  2. DraftingClaude Code turns it into up to six draft cards.
  3. ConceptenYou edit, listen, then accept or discard each draft.
  4. VoiceOn accept, the Dutch audio is made once and saved.
  5. OefenenThe card appears in study whenever it is due.

The cycle for every card: it is shown, you read it, you score it, you hear it. Each step waits for you. Pressing V at any step shows the English under the card.

  • Reading is its own step. You press Spatie to say you have read the card, and only then do the score buttons appear.
  • The app speaks only when you ask it to, after you have scored.
  • You can change your score after hearing the card, until you move to the next one.

Three modes, switched from the top of the screen:

  • Spreken: the cycle above. You read aloud, score yourself, then ask to hear it.
  • Stil: read and score only, with no sound, for the train or the office.
  • Luisteren: the audio plays first with the text hidden. You reveal the Dutch to check what you heard.

Toevoegen (add)

  • One large box. Paste anything: Dutch and English mixed, corrections, explanations.
  • An optional steering line, such as alleen de correcties or a different card limit.
  • Lines typed as Nederlands | English skip Claude and become drafts directly.

Concepten (drafts)

  • Each draft is a row with the Dutch, the note and the hidden English, all editable in place.
  • A play button lets you hear the Dutch before you accept it.
  • Accept, discard, or accept all.
  • A draft that matches a card already in the deck is flagged.
  • The original snippet stays attached so you can see where a card came from.

Kaarten (deck)

  • A searchable list of every card, filterable by tag and by date added.
  • Edit any field. Changing the Dutch regenerates the audio.
  • Suspend, delete, or reset a card's schedule.
  • Each row shows when the card is next due, how often you missed it, and how often you asked for the English.

What "Dutch only" means in practice

  • The face of every card is Dutch. There are no English-first cards.
  • Notes on a card are written in simple Dutch, for example: "Gaan" krijgt "zijn" in de voltooide tijd, niet "hebben".
  • The interface labels are in Dutch too.
  • The English translation is written when the card is drafted and shown only when you press V.
  • Each time you ask for the English, the card counts it. A card you keep peeking at is one you do not really know yet, and the deck screen shows that.

Scheduling

The four buttons feed FSRS, the scheduler modern Anki uses. It comes from an open-source library, so none of the maths is written by hand.

Weet ik nietDon't knowComes back later in the same session and starts again from a short gap
MoeilijkHardThe gap grows only a little
Gaat goedComfortableThe gap grows at the normal rate
MakkelijkEasyThe gap grows fastest
  • A daily limit on new cards, starting at 10, keeps a session short. You can change it.
  • Due cards come first, then new ones.

The voice

  • Piper speaks the cards. It runs on the laptop, costs nothing and has Dutch voices from the Netherlands and Flanders.
  • Each card's audio is generated once and saved as a file, so playback is instant and works offline.
  • Slow replay is a second recording spoken at a slower pace, so the pitch stays natural.
  • There is no online fallback. Without Piper, cards still work in silent mode.

Not yet done: I have not listened to the Dutch Piper voices. Choosing the best-sounding one is the first job of the voice phase.

Making cards from a snippet

The app hands your snippet to Claude Code in the background with a fixed set of rules and gets back a structured list of cards.

  • What it looks for, in order: corrections of your own mistakes, then new phrases, then single words placed in a short sentence.
  • Card rules: whole phrases a person would say, nouns always with de or het, one short Dutch note at most, and a hidden English translation.
  • Limit: six cards per snippet by default, so the deck stays worth reviewing.
  • Cost: each paste is one small request against your Claude subscription.
  • Spare: Codex is also installed here and can stand in if you would rather spend ChatGPT usage.

I have not timed a drafting request yet. Expect a wait of some seconds per paste, during which the screen shows that it is working.

Build order

Each step leaves you with something you can use.

  1. Interface experiment. One window in GPUI Community Edition that shows a card, takes typed text and plays a sound, built on this laptop. If that goes well the app uses it, and if not the app uses egui. The card logic is kept separate from the interface, so the choice can change later.
  2. Study loop. The deck, typed-in cards, the four grades, the scheduler, the Dutch interface and silent mode. You can study from the first day.
  3. Voice. Piper, saved audio, the reveal that speaks, slow replay and listening mode.
  4. Paste to drafts. The Add screen, drafting through Claude Code, and the Drafts review screen.
  5. Deck housekeeping. The card list, search, editing mid-review, suspend and reset, and the peek counts.
  6. Later, if you want them. Recording yourself to compare with the app's voice. Gap cards, where one word of a Dutch sentence is blanked, as a Dutch-only way to practise recall. A one-click backup.

Five assumptions to confirm

I made these choices to finish the plan. Each is easy to reverse before building starts.

  1. The interface labels are in Dutch, as well as the cards.
  2. Asking for the English is counted but does not change the grade you can give.
  3. The voice is from the Netherlands, with Flemish as an alternative.
  4. Notes are written in simple Dutch, with no English version kept.
  5. It is built in Rust with Turso as a desktop window. The interface uses GPUI Community Edition if the first experiment goes well, and egui if not.
